Yiddish Phonikud TTS

Hasidic (Unterland / Central) Yiddish — pointing, grapheme-to-phoneme, and speech, with the phoneme derivation shown for every word.

About the voice (blue-yi). This checkpoint is trained for Yiddish, not a multilingual model borrowed for it: its manifest declares yi alone, its latent statistics come from stats_yiddish.pt, and it was trained on IPA produced by this same phonikud-yi engine. Every unit of the closed inventory — including ʦ ʧ ʤ ɡ ŋ and the marks ˈ ː — is a single embedding in its vocabulary, so every phone reaches the model unfolded and none is dropped. (A few punctuation characters the engine passes through are outside the vocabulary and are removed silently; they carry no sound.) Two honest caveats. The four saved voices are LibriTTS-R readers of English, so the speaker identity carries a foreign colour even though the phonology is Yiddish; any 44.1 kHz recording can be used as a reference instead. And the duration predictor emits one total length rather than per-phoneme durations, with no monotonic alignment — when that total runs short the model compresses, and a word can be swallowed. If a word goes missing, generate again or shorten the sentence.
About the voice (Piper fallback). model.onnx is a Hebrew-trained Piper checkpoint being driven with Yiddish IPA: expect a Hebrew accent and no Yiddish prosody. It also has no ʧ or ʤ in its vocabulary, so those are folded to tʃ and dʒ and reported below. It runs at 22.05 kHz and is kept as the lightweight fallback.

Advanced — flow-matching controls

These two are blue-yi controls; the Piper fallback ignores them. n_steps (default 8) is how many flow-matching steps the vector estimator runs — more steps sound better and take longer. Measured on this machine for about 1.2 s of audio: 4 steps 121 ms, 8 steps 178 ms, 16 steps 295 ms. cfg_scale (default 4.0) is classifier-free guidance strength; the guidance is applied with a second pass per step, so 1.0 disables it and costs roughly half as much.
